# References

All sources were accessed on 2026-10-10. The assumption register ([assumption-register.md](assumption-register.md)) and the configuration (`configs/default.yaml`) cite them by the ID in brackets.

"Verified" means the quoted fact was read in the source itself. "Secondary" means it was confirmed through an independent source because the original was not accessible. Anything that could not be verified is not used as evidence in this project.

## Dataset

- <a id="kaggle2018"></a>**[kaggle2018]** BlastChar. (2018). *Telco Customer Churn* [Data set, version 1, released 2018-02-23]. Kaggle. https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/blastchar/telco-customer-churn. The page says "Customers who left within the last month – the column is called Churn". Licence shown: "Data files © Original Authors". Verified.
- <a id="ibmdocs"></a>**[ibmdocs]** IBM. (n.d.). *Telco customer churn* [IBM Cognos Analytics 11.1 sample documentation]. https://www.ibm.com/docs/SSEP7J_11.1.0/com.ibm.swg.ba.cognos.ig_smples.doc/c_telco_dm_sam.html. Describes "a fictional telco company's customer churn"; "the churn column indicates whether the customer departed within the last month." Verified.
- <a id="ibm2019"></a>**[ibm2019]** IBM Samples Team. (2019, July 11). *Telco customer churn (11.1.3+)*. IBM Community. https://community.ibm.com/community/user/businessanalytics/blogs/steven-macko/2019/07/11/telco-customer-churn-1113. Describes "a fictional telco company that provided home phone and Internet services to 7043 customers in California in Q3". In this extended version the churn label is "left the company this quarter". Verified.
- <a id="ibmgithub"></a>**[ibmgithub]** IBM. (2019). *telco-customer-churn-on-icp4d* [Computer software, Apache-2.0]. GitHub. https://github.com/IBM/telco-customer-churn-on-icp4d. Secondary download source used by the pipeline. Verified.
- <a id="kaggle2020ibm"></a>**[kaggle2020ibm]** TanKY. (2020). *Telco customer churn: IBM dataset* [Data set, version 1, updated 2020-12-07; "IBM Cognos Analytics 11.1.3+ base samples dataset"]. Kaggle. https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/yeanzc/telco-customer-churn-ibm-dataset. 7,043 customers and 33 columns, including Churn Reason and a CLTV index. The pipeline checks that customer IDs, churn labels and six other shared fields match the base file for every customer. IBM's own 11.1.3+ sample assigns the same reasons to five categories (Attitude, Competitor, Dissatisfaction, Price, Other), which this project uses. Licence shown: "Other (specified in description)". Verified.

## Profit-based churn management

- <a id="neslin2006"></a>**[neslin2006]** Neslin, S. A., Gupta, S., Kamakura, W., Lu, J., & Mason, C. H. (2006). Defection detection: Measuring and understanding the predictive accuracy of customer churn models. *Journal of Marketing Research, 43*(2), 204–211. https://doi.org/10.1509/jmkr.43.2.204. Gives the retention-campaign profit equation and uses a $50 incentive ("a month's service rebate"). It notes that "No published data are available for acceptance rate (γ), so we investigated 10%, 30%, and 50% rates." Verified.
- <a id="verbeke2012"></a>**[verbeke2012]** Verbeke, W., Dejaeger, K., Martens, D., Hur, J., & Baesens, B. (2012). New insights into churn prediction in the telecommunication sector: A profit driven data mining approach. *European Journal of Operational Research, 218*(1), 211–229. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejor.2011.09.031. Defines the maximum-profit criterion and sets γ, CLV, δ and c to 0.30, 200, 10 and 1 (EUR). Verified.
- <a id="verbraken2013"></a>**[verbraken2013]** Verbraken, T., Verbeke, W., & Baesens, B. (2013). A novel profit maximizing metric for measuring classification performance of customer churn prediction models. *IEEE Transactions on Knowledge and Data Engineering, 25*(5), 961–973. https://doi.org/10.1109/TKDE.2012.50. Expected maximum profit (EMPC) defaults: CLV 200, offer 10, contact 1, and acceptance γ ~ Beta(6, 14) with mean 0.30. Secondary (via Höppner et al., 2020, and the authors' `EMP` R package).
- <a id="hoppner2020"></a>**[hoppner2020]** Höppner, S., Stripling, E., Baesens, B., vanden Broucke, S., & Verdonck, T. (2020). Profit driven decision trees for churn prediction. *European Journal of Operational Research, 284*(3), 920–933. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejor.2018.11.072. Verified (arXiv preprint).
- <a id="lemmens2020"></a>**[lemmens2020]** Lemmens, A., & Gupta, S. (2020). Managing churn to maximize profits. *Marketing Science, 39*(5), 956–973. https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.2020.1229. Ranks customers on profit lift and separates conditional from unconditional incentives. Reports an offer cost of about EUR 12 per targeted customer, and "a North European telecom firm cited an average cost of approximately 7 euros per customer". Verified (accepted manuscript).
- <a id="burez2007"></a>**[burez2007]** Burez, J., & Van den Poel, D. (2007). CRM at a pay-TV company: Using analytical models to reduce customer attrition by targeted marketing for subscription services. *Expert Systems with Applications, 32*(2), 277–288. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eswa.2005.11.037. Abstract only.

## Targeting by response, and experiments

- <a id="ascarza2018"></a>**[ascarza2018]** Ascarza, E. (2018). Retention futility: Targeting high-risk customers might be ineffective. *Journal of Marketing Research, 55*(1), 80–98. https://doi.org/10.1509/jmr.16.0163. In a randomised prepaid-wireless study (N = 12,137), targeting the 40% most sensitive to the offer cut churn by 6.0 points, against 1.9 points when targeting by risk. Verified.
- <a id="ascarza2016"></a>**[ascarza2016]** Ascarza, E., Iyengar, R., & Schleicher, M. (2016). The perils of proactive churn prevention using plan recommendations: Evidence from a field experiment. *Journal of Marketing Research, 53*(1), 46–60. https://doi.org/10.1509/jmr.13.0483. Churn was 10% among treated customers against 6% in the control group. Verified.
- <a id="ascarza2018review"></a>**[ascarza2018review]** Ascarza, E., Neslin, S. A., Netzer, O., et al. (2018). In pursuit of enhanced customer retention management: Review, key issues, and future directions. *Customer Needs and Solutions, 5*(1–2), 65–81. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40547-017-0080-0. Verified (abstract).
- <a id="devriendt2021"></a>**[devriendt2021]** Devriendt, F., Berrevoets, J., & Verbeke, W. (2021). Why you should stop predicting customer churn and start using uplift models. *Information Sciences, 548*, 497–515. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ins.2019.12.075. Verified.
- <a id="kohavi2020"></a>**[kohavi2020]** Kohavi, R., Tang, D., & Xu, Y. (2020). *Trustworthy online controlled experiments: A practical guide to A/B testing*. Cambridge University Press. https://doi.org/10.1017/9781108653985. Cited for the design of randomised experiments and holdout groups; no quotations are used.

## Customer value

On the discount rate: "Finance texts generally suggest a range of 8% to 16% … we use the average of 12%." Verified.
- <a id="faderhardie2014"></a>**[faderhardie2014]** Fader, P. S., & Hardie, B. G. S. (2014). *What's wrong with this CLV formula?* http://brucehardie.com/notes/033/. A finite-horizon value should be called "truncated CLV"; retention tends to rise with tenure. Verified.
